Both offline modes need no outbound access at runtime, but installing them still
takes artifacts from Fish Audio: the images and, on Helm, the chart. An air-gapped
deployment is about getting those across the boundary, together with the license
bundle and the reference voices you use. The license expires, so plan a recurring
transfer, not a one-off; voices are copied in as archives, since voice sync cannot
reach Fish Audio from there.

<Note>
  Fish Audio does this with you as part of an air-gapped delivery, and the step-by-step
  procedure is in the deployment guide for the version you install. This page is
  what to expect and what to plan for.
</Note>

## Which one to choose

**The air-gapped All-in-One appliance** is the straightforward answer to a strict air
gap: one image, moved to the disconnected host as a file and loaded there. Beyond the
image, the license bundle — and, in the external-weights modes, the weight
fileset — has to be carried across. Its online counterpart cannot run air
gapped: it authorizes every request against Fish Audio.

**The Helm chart** is more work, because the release is many images rather than one.
Every image has to be mirrored into a registry the cluster can reach and the release
pointed at it, component by component. Plan for the whole
set, and expect to do it with your account team rather than alone.

## Offline usage accounting

Offline billing records usage in a signed ledger on persistent storage. It is durable
data, not cache: back it up, and agree with your account team how often to send it for
reconciliation.

## Proving there is no egress

Regulated deployments usually need evidence rather than a configuration review, and
your deployment guide carries the exercise. It shows both that the deployment works
without the internet and that, with voice sync off, it makes no calls when the internet
is available. On Helm
it is a cluster-level exercise, so involve whoever runs your cluster early: a
namespace-scoped account cannot complete it.
