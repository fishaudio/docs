Self-hosted images, charts, and documentation are distributed from a private Fish Audio
registry. Your team authenticates to it with a **deploy token** created in the fish.audio
dashboard.

## The Self Host page

Sign in to fish.audio and open **Developer → Self Host**. It lists your team's deploy
tokens and deployments, each deployment with its model and billing mode. A deployment's
page gives its license, the environment it needs, and install commands for the version
you pick, with the registry host and artifact references filled in. The install step
also downloads that version's **deployment guide**: the Helm deployment runbook or the
All-in-One guide that the other pages here refer to. Each model has its own versions,
and a deployment stays on its version until you install another. If the page says
self-hosting is not enabled, or nothing has been granted to your team yet, contact your
account manager.

## Prerequisites

- Self-hosting enabled for your team under an enterprise agreement.
- A fish.audio account that is a member of that team.
- Docker for the All-in-One, and for mirroring images into an air-gapped cluster;
  Helm 3.8 or newer for the Kubernetes chart.

## Deploy tokens

Create one token per consumer, such as a production cluster, a staging cluster, or a CI
mirror, and name it after that consumer. The value is shown once: store it in your
secret manager, not in values files or version control. A team holds up to five tokens,
and they carry the team's grants rather than their creator's.

Docker, Helm, and the cluster's image pull secret all sign in with your account email
and the token.

Rotating or deleting a token revokes the old value at once. Running pods keep running,
but new pulls, and an Online deployment's license renewals, fail until every consumer
has a valid token. To switch with no gap, create a second token, move every consumer to
it as your deployment guide shows, then delete the old one. The token list shows when
each token was last used, so you can retire the ones nothing uses.

## The license

The license is the product permit: every deployment has its own, in the install bundle
you download from its page. Without it, the services never come up. Once it expires,
they refuse requests (403) until a renewed one is in place.

The license renews each billing cycle. Online and Networked Ledger deployments renew
their own, and stop serving after a week without a successful sync. An air-gapped one is
renewed once its ledger passes Fish Audio's check; you then install the new license by
hand, as your deployment guide shows.
